# 26: Operational Reporting and Aggregate Oversight

Coordinators and supervisors need to understand what is happening across many workers, places, and activities.

They ask questions such as: how many reports have arrived, which locations have not submitted, which items are pending review, which activities are delayed, and how old the available information is.

Reports are used for operational decisions, not just historical summaries. A coordinator may redirect workers, follow up with supervisors, or decide whether an activity is ready to close.

This results in:

* Aggregate views of progress, missing work, and pending review
* Visibility into how fresh or stale the reported data is
* Clear treatment of questionable or unresolved records
* Reports scoped to what each viewer is allowed to see

---

## What makes this hard

Field data is not always current. Reports based on synced data may be hours or days behind reality. Decision-makers need to see the age and completeness of the information rather than treating the report as live truth.

Unresolved issues affect numbers. A count that includes questionable records may answer a different question than a count that excludes them. Reports should make that distinction visible.

Different viewers have different scopes. A district supervisor, regional coordinator, and auditor may ask similar questions but should not necessarily see the same underlying records.

Aggregate views can hide accountability. A high-level number should be traceable back to the records and responsibility chain that produced it when someone needs to investigate.
