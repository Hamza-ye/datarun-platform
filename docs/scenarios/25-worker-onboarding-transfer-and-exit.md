# 25: Worker Onboarding, Transfer, Leave, and Exit

A new worker joins an operational team. They receive responsibility for a defined set of places, subjects, or activities and prepare their device before going to the field.

Later, responsibilities change. A worker may transfer to another area, cover for someone temporarily, go on leave, or exit the organization. Their ongoing work needs to be handed off without losing what has already happened.

Some changes happen while devices are offline. A worker may continue acting under the last responsibilities they knew about, then reconnect after central assignments have changed.

This results in:

* New workers receiving the data and responsibilities needed to start work
* Responsibility transfers that preserve continuity
* Work done during stale or temporary responsibility windows remaining traceable
* Successor workers seeing enough context to continue the work
* Former workers no longer carrying information they should not retain

---

## What makes this hard

Authority is temporary and contextual. A person may be allowed to do one kind of work in one area but not another. Transfers must change current authority without rewriting what was true at the time earlier work was performed.

Offline devices may act on stale knowledge. A worker who has not yet learned that their responsibility ended may still capture work. That work should not disappear, but it should be visible that authority had changed centrally.

Handoff requires enough history to be useful. A successor cannot continue work if they only receive future assignments and no context about what happened before.

Exit is not just disabling login. The organization must preserve attribution for what the person did while also preventing continuing access after their role ends.
