# 12: When Something Happens, Something Else Should Follow

Certain observations or occurrences should automatically lead to a response. A stockout is reported — someone should investigate. A critical value is recorded — the right people should know. A deadline passes without action — attention should be drawn to it.

The response may involve different people than those who recorded the original information. Some situations escalate: if the initial response doesn't happen, a broader or more urgent reaction follows.

This results in:

* Responses that follow from what has been observed or recorded
* The right people becoming aware when something significant happens
* Escalation when expected responses don't occur in time

---

## What makes this hard

The original observation may be recorded while the worker is offline. The response cannot always happen at the moment of observation; it may only become possible when the information reaches someone with connectivity. People using the response need to know that delay existed.

The same situation may be recorded more than once by different people. A response should not multiply uncontrollably just because overlapping records arrive later.

Some observations are questionable until reviewed. If the original record is disputed, incomplete, or outside the worker's current responsibility, follow-up work should not blindly proceed as if the record were unquestionably valid.

Escalation has to be targeted. Too many automatic responses can overwhelm supervisors, while too few can leave serious issues unattended.
